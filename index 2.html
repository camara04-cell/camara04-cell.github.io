Project 01 / Healthcare data analysis

Chicago Hospital Quality Analysis

Explored publicly available Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) hospital data to examine overall quality ratings and patient safety measure classifications for rated Chicago hospitals. Cleaned and validated data in Excel, then reproduced key comparisons with SQL.

How the analysis was conducted

  1. Selected the study population. Started with 33 Chicago hospital records and retained 22 with available overall CMS star ratings; 11 unrated hospitals were excluded from rating comparisons. The rated set includes 21 acute care hospitals and one VA hospital.
  2. Prepared and checked the data in Excel. Standardized an analysis-ready table and verified that Better, No Different, and Worse counts sum to the reported safety measure total for each hospital.
  3. Analyzed the data in SQLite. Ran row counts, rating distributions, grouped safety comparisons, hospital-level filters, and aggregate validation queries.
  4. Cross-checked the outputs. SQL returned 117 classifications: 24 Better, 91 No Different, and 2 Worse, matching the Excel calculations.

Interpretation notes: CMS safety classifications compare facility performance with national benchmarks. They are not counts of incidents or patient outcomes. The 117 observations are facility-level reported classifications, not 117 distinct safety measures. Hospitals report different numbers of measures. Results are descriptive, based on the selected dataset, and do not establish causation or generalize to all Chicago hospitals.

See a sample SQL query

The query below compares the combined share of Better safety classifications for 1-star and 5-star hospitals.

SELECT overall_rating,
       COUNT(*) AS hospital_count,
       SUM(safety_better) AS better_measures,
       SUM(safety_total) AS total_measures,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(safety_better)
             / NULLIF(SUM(safety_total), 0), 1)
         AS better_percentage
FROM hospitals
WHERE overall_rating IN ('1', '5')
GROUP BY overall_rating
ORDER BY overall_rating;

Expected output: 1 star → 7 hospitals, 3.6%; 5 stars → 2 hospitals, 56.3%.
